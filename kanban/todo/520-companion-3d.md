# The companion in 3D

**Problem:** the companion only exists flat. A polished 3D version of him (stills and a short acted loop) would give the README, the store and the demo video a picture that feels alive, and it's fun.

**Evidence of demand:** the maintainer asked for it while designing the companion (card 240): "build a highly polished 3d version of our mascot", aiming for feature-animation quality. The eyes were reworked against the plush toy and approved (v41, "Nice"); the final renders are still to do.

**Approach:** the Blender script below. Check its eyes against the official 2D look first (they were tuned to the toy before the 2D eyes were levelled), then render the finals (stills at 2048px, 1024 samples; the loop at 1080px, 192 samples, about two hours), re-render the lid close-ups with the sunk eyes, encode the loop (frames 1–95), and update the "Companion in 3D" page (https://claude.ai/artifact/Xg1LaLd26ifFvkBResS8uF). Decide with the maintainer whether any of it goes into `brand/`, and where it shows (README, store, demo video).

**Done when:**
- [ ] The final stills and loop are rendered and on the page.

**A 3D companion, for fun** (user: "build a highly polished 3d version of our mascot"). Not part of this card's deliverable; kept here so the choices aren't lost. Built in Blender 5.2 from one script (`build/brand/3d/build.py`, run headless: `blender -b --factory-startup --python build.py -- [res=… spp=… yaw=…]`), rendered in Cycles on the GPU.
- Body: the bun from the 2D drawing as a superellipsoid (wider than tall, a flatter bottom), sitting on the floor. Eyes: clean ovals with a clear glossy coat over a matte white, standing proud of the fur.
- Fur: about 1.2 million strands in four layers (underfur, guard hairs in clumps that make the 2D drawing's spiky outline, a fine frizzy halo, and the crown tuft). Clumped harder on the outline than on the face, so the face reads plush. Clumps at 0.86 looked like wet paintbrushes and quills up close; 0.76 with a gentle per-strand bend reads fluffy.
- Colour: Slides yellow behind him, kept true by using the Standard view (AgX and PBR Neutral both washed it out) and measuring the backdrop's pixels against #FFCF03. The fur absorbs every channel equally (melanin turned backlit tips purple). The lights behind him are tinted towards the yellow, as if the backdrop bounced back: white rims made the black tips read as grey hair.
- Lights are linked by object (fur, eyes, set), so the eyes get their own catchlights (round, softer than the rectangles first tried) without lighting the fur twice.
- Exports are 16-bit; 8-bit copies are dithered, or the floor's shadow bands into rings.
- Catchlights are cheated per eye and per camera, as lighters do: each eye has its own lamp, placed by reflection so its highlight sits on the upper left of that pupil from whichever camera renders (one lamp for both put it on one pupil and the other eye's white).
- **Eyes: close together, set into the fur.** References: the original drawing, then the soot-sprite plush with domed eyes. The user's call after seeing both: our closer spacing, and the toy's deeper, inset eyes. Kept from the toy: about 0.24 and 0.23 of the fur's width at eye height, about 1.2 times as tall as wide, the right eye a little narrower and higher (a fifth to a quarter of an eye), the left upright and the right leaning its top left (tilted 7° and 8° in the model, since the body's curve leans them the other way on screen), pupils about a quarter of the eye, turned in towards each other so he looks at you. Spacing as in the closer version: a narrow ridge of fur between the eyes. Set per eye with `L=` / `R=` (x, z, rx, rz, pupil x, pupil z, tilt).
- **Why the eyes stood out like eggs.** A sign slip from the first version placed each eye's centre in front of the skin instead of behind it, so the eyes floated on the fur (and open lids showed round them like goggles). Each eye's centre now sits 40% of its depth behind the skin (`sink=`), and a ring of soft fur rooted round each eye leans in and drapes over its edge, resting on the eyeball (`rimlean`, `rimlen`): the toy's look. Combing the face fur alone, or letting it reach over the edge, did nothing while the eyes floated.
- **Lids, not painted bands** (user: the old blink, and the 2D one, aren't good enough; aim for feature-animation quality). Each eye has an upper and a lower lid: thick shells just outside the eyeball, turning about its horizontal axis, so they slide over it. Their margins dip towards the corners (the same on both, so they meet all along when shut): the upper lid's edge arches like a real one, and a rising lower lid arches up into a smile. A straight lower margin read as a sleepy visor. The lids are furred like the body (short strands in tufts) so a shut eye is the creature's own skin closing, and get a dimmer copy of the key and fill: as furry domes standing out into the light they read as grey wool. A fully open lid is hidden, so it can never show as a ring round the eye.
- **The 4-second loop, acted** (96 frames at 24 fps, 1080px, motion blur at half a frame): he looks at you; something up to his left catches his eye (a saccade: two frames, a hair of overshoot) and his body turns and leans after it a few frames later; he blinks as he brings his eyes back to you (a blink hides a change of gaze) and opens them into a smile, the lower lids rising while the upper ones stay open (a lowered upper lid read as sleepy); a small hop with anticipation, stretch, a parabola, squash on landing and a squeeze of the eyes on impact; he settles. Breathing throughout. The blink: shut in three frames, held two, open over six, the lower lid half a frame behind, the body dipping slightly with it. Without motion blur the lid's fastest frames strobe.
- The fur is the same on every run (seed 7, picked from four for the most even crown). bmesh's triangulate returned triangles in a different order each run, which reshuffled every hair; they're now sorted. Cycles 5.2 crashes on frame 2 with persistent data and this much hair, so the loop rebuilds the scene each frame (about 70 s a frame on an M1 Max with motion blur).
- Outputs, in `build/brand/3d/` (gitignored, so local only): front and three-quarter stills at 2048px (16-bit PNGs, dithered 8-bit PNGs, JPGs), the loop as MP4 and GIF, the script and the front `.blend`. The video stops at frame 95, since frame 96 matches frame 1 and would stutter at the seam. A page with the stills, the loop and close-ups is published as the "Companion in 3D" artifact.
